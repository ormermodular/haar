HAAR

USER MANUAL · EXPERT SLEEPERS DISTING NT

Haar is the sea fog that swallows the Channel Islands in an afternoon. The reverb of the same name is a lush stereo algorithmic tail with that weather baked in: highs die into the murk, lows bloom. Three knobs do almost everything. FOG is how much weather, GLOW is the lamp warming through it, DRIFT is how much the world moves.

This is the live display, running the same drawing code as the plug-in. Fog thickens with FOG and the stars fade behind it. The lamp and its halo heat up with GLOW. DRIFT is the weather: swell rolls through the sea, the beam speeds up and hunts, and past halfway the tape brings grain, dropouts and a slow weave of the whole picture. The spray sparkle follows the actual wet level. The three labels along the bottom sit above the three physical pots.

The three knobs

Under the knobs is a full algorithmic plate: predelay, drive, a diffusion tank with split high/low damping, a tilt EQ and a tape stage. You never meet those parameters raw. Each knob is a macro: a curated path through the engine, tuned by ear against a low foghorn tone.

IN→PREDELAY→DRIVE→ DIFFUSION TANK→TILT EQ→TAPE / LO-FI→MIX→OUT

FOG: how much weather

Size, decay and diffusion ride together. The tail is fully smeared by 30%, because Haar has no interest in discrete early reflections, and noon is already a proper hall, around five to six seconds. Size glides rather than jumps, so riding FOG bends the tail's pitch tape-style. The damping asymmetry (aggressive on top, light below) is the fixed character, deepening slightly with size the way air eats treble over distance.

The last few degrees are freeze. Above roughly 85% the loop gain ramps to unity: the lows hold and bloom indefinitely while the high damping keeps eating the top, so a frozen chord slowly sinks into the murk instead of ringing static.

GLOW: the lamp

Drive into the tank, with a darkening tilt EQ riding alongside so the saturation thickens instead of turning harsh. The distortion is asymmetric, which gives even harmonics and warmth rather than buzz. The loop's own saturation deepens with it, so the tail itself compresses and warms, not just the signal entering it.

DRIFT: how much the world moves

Still water at zero, though a whisper of motion is always on because nothing analog is pitch-perfect. The lower half brings up four sine LFOs into a slow lush chorus, at its deepest around 45%. From there genuine random wander takes over and the tape comes in: wow first, flutter and deeper saturation past 60%, and a real top-end rolloff that pulls the tail down toward 2 kHz at full. The sine chorus recedes as the weather worsens but never leaves.

Feel: big DRIFT moves settle over a fraction of a second. Weather changes, it doesn't switch. As the tape engages you may hear a brief pitch swoop while the tape distance stretches in. Both are deliberate. With GLOW and DRIFT both near full the tape layer and the drive stack up and the tail turns dirty on purpose: that corner is heavy weather.

Other controls

ControlRangeWhat it does
Input0–100%Level into the tank. Interacts with GLOW: hotter input drives harder. To keep the tank clean, pull Input and GLOW back and make the level up with Wet level.
Mix0–100%Equal-power dry/wet. 100% is the classic send-return wash.
Predelay0–500 msSpace before the fog arrives. It glides when changed, bending pitch tape-style rather than clicking.
Filter HP0–100%High-pass on the wet path, sweeping up to about 900 Hz. Thin the fog, keep the shimmer. Exactly open at 0, click-free at any sweep speed.
Filter LP0–100%Low-pass on the wet path, sweeping down to about 900 Hz. Sink the tail below the mix. Independent of HP: both together leave a band of fog.
Shimmer0–100%Pitch-shifted regeneration: a shifted tap of the tail feeds back into the tank, so each pass climbs (or falls). Haar's high damping eats every generation, so the voices bloom, then sink into the murk. Safe with freeze: it holds and glows rather than running away.
Shim tune−24 – +24 stThe shimmer interval, default +12 (octave up). −12 is the sleeper: the fog deepening instead of glowing. Retuning a ringing tail bends it varispeed-style. Octaves stay in key through every generation; other intervals stack as they recirculate (a fifth blooms into a spiral of fifths). That wander is the weather, not a fault.
Wet level0.0–12.0 dBMake-up gain on the wet path, after the tank and before the mix. Pull Input and GLOW back to keep the tank clean and give it headroom, then push the tail back up here. At 0 dB (the default) Haar sounds exactly as it always did, and the dry signal is never touched. It has no pot: set it from the parameter page, or map it.

On the disting NT

Installing

Haar needs disting NT firmware 1.19 or newer.

  1. Download haar-plugin.zip and unzip it onto the root of the MicroSD card. It puts haar.o in programs/plug-ins/ormermodular/.
  2. Restart the module (or rescan plug-ins from the settings menu).
  3. Add an algorithm → Haar (tagged Effect / Reverb).

The pots: three pages

The three pots always do exactly three things, and which three depends on the page. Pushing the centre pot steps to the next page; the label strip along the bottom of the screen always names what the pots do right now, with a level bar for each.

Nothing ever jumps: after a page change, each pot stays silent until you turn it to the value it now owns (the NT's soft takeover). Flip pages freely mid-performance. Wet level has no pot, so it is not on any page: set it from the parameter page.

Automation and CV

Every parameter, the three macros included, is a normal NT parameter, so all of it can be mapped to CV, MIDI or i2c and automated through the module's own Mappings system, exactly like a built-in algorithm. The macros run at 0.1% resolution and every audible target glides inside the engine, so mapped steps and fast automation stay clean.

Field notes

Foghorn

The reference patch. A low, slow drone becomes a coastline.

FOG 65 · GLOW 40 · DRIFT 15 · MIX 60
Feed it anything dark and sustained; let the asymmetric damping do the arranging.

Held light

Freeze as an instrument. Catch a chord, then play against the fog.

FOG 100 · GLOW 25 · DRIFT 10 · MIX 100
Map FOG to a CV source and drop it from 100 to ~70 to let the frozen tail exhale.

Worn tape

The full weather: wow, flutter, grain, the top gone soft.

FOG 55 · GLOW 60 · DRIFT 85 · MIX 70
Best on melodic loops: the wander detunes the tail against the dry.

Undertow

Down-shimmer: the tail keeps sinking an octave into the murk.

FOG 70 · SHIMMER 55 · Shim tune −12 · MIX 70
Slow melodic lines grow a seabed. Try Shim tune −7 for something stranger.

Lantern choir

Shimmer through fog: octaves that bloom above a drone and sink back into it.

FOG 75 · GLOW 30 · DRIFT 10 · SHIMMER 60 · MIX 80
Push FOG to the top mid-swell and the choir hangs in the frozen fog.

Harbour room

Short, warm, barely moving. Haar as a mixer's space rather than an effect.

FOG 20 · GLOW 30 · DRIFT 0 · MIX 25
Add 40–80 ms of predelay to keep transients ahead of the wash.

Troubleshooting

Haar doesn't appear in the NT's algorithm list.
Update the module's firmware (Haar needs 1.19 or newer) and check haar.o really is inside programs/plug-ins/ormermodular/.
I hear the dry signal twice.
The output mode is set to Add while Mix is below 100%. Either keep the default Replace, or run Mix fully wet when summing onto a bus.
The tail pitch-bends when I move FOG.
That's the size glide: the tank physically stretches, like varispeed on tape. Ride it slowly for a chorus-like bloom, or park it before a take.
Frozen tails eventually go dark.
By design: freeze holds the lows at unity while high damping keeps working, so a frozen chord sinks into the murk rather than ringing static.
